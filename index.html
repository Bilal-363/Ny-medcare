What's Included

What happens during your I-693 exam

USCIS and the CDC set the exact scope of the immigration medical examination. Here is what a complete exam covers, and what we perform at NY MedCare.

Physical Examination

A head-to-toe examination including eyes, ears, nose and throat, heart, lungs, abdomen, lymph nodes, skin and extremities, plus height, weight, blood pressure and vision.

Tuberculosis Screening (IGRA)

We order an Interferon Gamma Release Assay blood test, required by the CDC for applicants aged 2 and older. The older PPD skin test is no longer accepted as the initial screen.

Required Blood & Urine Labs

Syphilis serology and gonorrhea testing within the CDC-specified age ranges. Ordered at your visit and processed by the laboratory we direct you to.

Vaccination Record Review

We review your immunization history against the CDC schedule, identify exactly what is missing for your age, and write the prescriptions so you can complete them at your pharmacy.

Mental Health & Substance Screening

A confidential evaluation for mental-health conditions associated with harmful behavior and for drug or alcohol use disorders, as USCIS requires.

Completed & Sealed Form I-693

Dr. Irshad completes and signs the current 01/20/2025 edition, seals it in the required envelope, and gives you a duplicate copy to keep.

If your TB blood test comes back positive

A positive IGRA does not mean you have active tuberculosis, and it does not disqualify you. It does mean a chest X-ray and further evaluation are required before your form can be completed. We coordinate imaging locally and walk you through every step.

Come Prepared

What to bring, and which vaccines USCIS requires

Arriving with the right paperwork is the single biggest thing you can do to keep your file moving — it is what lets Dr. Irshad write the right orders the first time.

Bring to your appointment

Missing an item rarely stops the exam, but it can delay your sealed form.

  • Government-issued photo ID — passport, driver's license or state ID.
  • Form I-693, edition 01/20/2025 — printed, with Part 1 completed by you. USCIS has accepted only this edition since July 3, 2025.
  • All vaccination records — including records from your home country, however old.
  • USCIS receipt notice or A-number, if one has been issued to you.
  • List of current medications and any relevant medical or surgical history.
  • Prior TB testing or treatment records — including any past chest X-ray reports.
  • An interpreter, if you would be more comfortable with one.
  • Payment method — immigration exams are typically self-pay. We quote the price before you book.

Vaccines USCIS may require

Which ones apply depends on your age and immunization history. We identify exactly what you need and write the prescription.

MMR (Measles, Mumps, Rubella) Tdap / Td Polio (IPV) Varicella (Chickenpox) Hepatitis A Hepatitis B Haemophilus influenzae type b Rotavirus Meningococcal Pneumococcal Seasonal Influenza Titers (proof of immunity)
Two recent changes worth knowing. Polio (IPV) was added to the CDC technical instructions for civil surgeons in May 2024. The COVID-19 vaccination requirement was removed by USCIS effective January 22, 2025 — you no longer need to document it on Form I-693.

Lost your vaccination records? You still have options.

This is one of the most common situations we see. Depending on your age and history we either prescribe the vaccines you need or order blood titers to prove you are already immune. Either route completes the vaccination section of your form.

Questions

Immigration exam FAQ

The questions applicants ask us most often, answered plainly.

What exactly is Form I-693?

Form I-693, the Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record, is the medical report USCIS requires from most green card applicants. Only a USCIS-designated civil surgeon may perform the exam and sign the form. It confirms that you are free of certain communicable diseases of public health significance and that your vaccinations meet CDC requirements.

Is Dr. Irshad a USCIS-designated civil surgeon?

Yes. Dr. Huma Irshad is a USCIS-designated civil surgeon and performs Form I-693 immigration medical examinations at NY MedCare in Manhattan, New York. You can also verify any civil surgeon's designation through the USCIS "Find a Doctor" tool.

How long does the appointment take?

Plan on about an hour for the exam visit itself — the physical examination, your vaccination history review, and the lab orders and prescriptions Dr. Irshad writes for you. Your testing is processed by an outside laboratory; results typically return in 3 to 5 business days, and your sealed Form I-693 is ready as soon as they do.

Do I need a TB skin test or a blood test?

A blood test. CDC technical instructions require an Interferon Gamma Release Assay (IGRA) blood test for applicants aged 2 and older. The older PPD skin test is no longer accepted as the initial screen. We write the order at your visit and direct you to the collection site. If your IGRA is positive, a chest X-ray and an evaluation for active tuberculosis are required before the form can be completed — a positive result alone does not disqualify you.

Which vaccinations does USCIS require?

Requirements follow the CDC immunization schedule and depend on your age. They commonly include MMR, Tdap or Td, polio (IPV), varicella, hepatitis A and B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, rotavirus, meningococcal, pneumococcal and seasonal influenza. Polio was added to the technical instructions in May 2024, and the COVID-19 vaccination requirement was removed effective January 22, 2025. We identify exactly which ones you still need and write the prescriptions so you can complete them at your pharmacy.

What if I have lost my vaccination records?

Very common, and not a dead end. Depending on your age and history we either prescribe the vaccines you need or order blood titers to document existing immunity. Either path lets us complete the vaccination section of Form I-693.

Does my completed Form I-693 expire?

Under USCIS guidance effective June 11, 2025, a Form I-693 signed by a civil surgeon on or after November 1, 2023 remains valid while the application it was filed with is pending. USCIS has revised this guidance more than once, so confirm the current rule with USCIS or your immigration attorney before you file.

Which edition of the form should I bring?

Bring the 01/20/2025 edition. Since July 3, 2025 USCIS accepts only that edition. Download it fresh from uscis.gov rather than reusing an old printout, and complete Part 1 yourself before you arrive — leave the rest for the civil surgeon.

Can I open the sealed envelope?

No. USCIS will reject a Form I-693 that arrives in an opened or tampered envelope. We seal and sign the envelope for you and hand you a separate unsealed copy for your records, so you never have a reason to break the seal.

Does insurance cover the immigration physical?

Usually not. Most plans exclude immigration medical examinations because USCIS requires them for an immigration benefit rather than for medical treatment. We give you a clear self-pay price before you book so there are no surprise bills. Call (917) 905-8140 for current pricing.

Do you serve applicants from outside Manhattan?

Yes. We regularly see applicants from all five boroughs — Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Staten Island — as well as Westchester, Long Island and northern New Jersey.

Do I need to be an existing patient or have a referral?

Neither. The immigration medical exam is a stand-alone service. You do not need a referral, you do not need to be an established patient, and you are under no obligation to transfer your primary care to us afterward.

Can my whole family be seen on the same day?

Yes. Each applicant needs their own exam and their own Form I-693, but we routinely schedule families back to back so everyone is done in one trip. Book each person a slot on the calendar above, or call us and we will arrange the block for you.
